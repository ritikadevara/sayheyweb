Why we exist

Gen Z has been called the “loneliest generation.” In 2023 the U.S. Surgeon General named loneliness a public health epidemic.

Loneliness doesn't just feel bad. It's been linked to heart disease, dementia, anxiety and more. And with fewer third spaces and more of our social lives on screens, the small, warm moments that used to pull strangers into conversation are getting rarer.

We're medical students. We think a lot about health, and we think connection is part of it. So we made something small, cheap and analog: a pin that makes saying hey a little less awkward. No app. No profile. No algorithm.

How it works

1

Grab a pin

Find us at campus tabling events around DC, or become a Connector and we'll get one to you.

2

Wear it

Bag, jacket, backpack strap, lanyard. Anywhere people can see it on the metro, in line for coffee, or in the elevator.

3

Say hey!

See someone wearing one? They're open to chat. Say hi, ask about their day, compliment their tote. That's it.

The (very short) rulebook

  • It's an invitation, not an obligation. Anyone can say “not right now,” and that's okay.
  • Keep it kind and keep it light. Start with the small stuff.
  • Respect the vibe. Headphones in, rushing to class? Try again another time.
  • Pass it on. Made a friend? Get them a pin.

Not sure what to say?

“What's the best thing you've eaten this week?”
